# Tidewell Residential Waste Guide - Knowledge Base

**Organization:** Tidewell Waste & Recycling Services (fictional)  
**Department:** Residential Waste Guidance  
**Service area:** houses, townhomes and apartment buildings in the town of Tidewell  
**Purpose:** Help residents decide how to sort, prepare and dispose of everyday household waste correctly.  
**Contact:** Residents can contact the waste service through its website, its mobile app or its customer service line.  
**Out of scope:** Commercial, industrial and construction waste are not covered by the residential guide.

Facts are deliberately generic: no street addresses, phone numbers, staff names or clock times.

## Disposal streams

### Blue recycling cart (`recycling`, curbside)

Clean paper, cardboard, rigid plastic containers, metal cans, foil, glass bottles and jars, and cartons.

- Everything must be empty, clean and dry.
- Put recyclables in loose; never bag them.
- Put caps and lids back on bottles, jugs and jars.
- Flatten cardboard boxes so they fit with the lid closed.

### Green organics cart (`organics`, curbside)

All food scraps, food-soiled paper, and yard and garden trimmings.

- All food scraps are accepted, including meat, bones and dairy.
- No plastic of any kind, including bags labelled compostable.
- Line the kitchen pail with newspaper or a paper bag if you want to.

### Black garbage cart (`garbage`, curbside)

Items that cannot be recycled, composted or dropped off, such as soft plastic, foam, diapers and pet waste.

- Bag garbage before putting it in the cart.
- The lid must close; extra bags beside the cart may not be collected.
- Never put hazardous waste, batteries or electronics in the garbage.

### Household hazardous waste drop-off (`hazardous`, drop-off)

Products that are flammable, corrosive, toxic or explosive, plus batteries and mercury-containing items.

- Never put hazardous waste in any curbside cart or pour it down a drain.
- Bring it to a community drop-off depot or a household hazardous waste collection event.
- Keep products in their original, labelled containers with lids sealed.
- Small household quantities are accepted free of charge.

### Electronics drop-off (`electronics`, drop-off)

Small items with a plug, cord or battery.

- Anything with a plug, cord or battery is electronic waste and never goes in a curbside cart.
- Bring it to a community drop-off depot or a retailer take-back program.
- Wipe personal data from devices before dropping them off.

### Bulky item collection (`bulky`, scheduled pickup)

Items too large to fit in a cart, such as furniture, mattresses and large appliances.

- Houses must book a bulky item pickup in advance; do not leave items at the curb without a booking.
- Apartment and condo residents should use their building's designated bulky item area and check with the property manager first.
- Usable furniture and appliances can be donated instead.

### Textile donation or drop-off (`textiles`, drop-off)

Clothing, shoes, linens and other fabric items.

- Donate wearable and usable items.
- Worn-out or torn textiles go to a textile drop-off bin, not the recycling cart.
- Textiles must be clean, dry and in a tied bag.

### Pharmacy take-back (`takeback`, drop-off)

Medications and medical sharps.

- Return unused or expired medications to a pharmacy.
- Never flush medications or put them in any cart.
- Sharps must be in a sealed, puncture-resistant container.

## Material rules

| id | stream | rule |
|---|---|---|
| R01 | recycling | Clean paper and cardboard go in the blue recycling cart. |
| R02 | organics | Paper that is soiled with food or grease goes in the green organics cart. |
| R03 | recycling | Rigid plastic containers such as bottles, jugs, tubs and clamshells go in the blue recycling cart once they are empty and rinsed. |
| R04 | garbage | Soft or stretchy plastic such as bags, wrap, wrappers and pouches goes in the garbage, never in recycling. |
| R05 | recycling | Metal cans, tins and clean aluminum foil go in the blue recycling cart. |
| R06 | recycling | Glass bottles and jars go in the blue recycling cart. |
| R07 | garbage | Glass that is not a bottle or jar, such as drinking glasses, window glass and ovenware, goes in the garbage, wrapped for safety. |
| R08 | organics | All food scraps, including meat, bones and dairy, go in the green organics cart. |
| R09 | organics | Yard and garden waste such as leaves, grass and small branches goes in the green organics cart. |
| R10 | electronics | Small items with a plug, cord or battery go to an electronics drop-off. |
| R11 | hazardous | Batteries of every kind go to a household hazardous waste drop-off, never in a cart. |
| R12 | hazardous | Products labelled flammable, corrosive, toxic or poison go to a household hazardous waste drop-off. |
| R13 | bulky | Items too large to fit in a cart with the lid closed, including large appliances, are bulky items. |
| R14 | textiles | Clothing and fabric items are donated or taken to a textile drop-off. |
| R15 | garbage | Foam packaging, such as takeout containers and packing peanuts, goes in the garbage. |
| R16 | takeback | Medications and needles are returned through pharmacy take-back. |
| R17 | garbage | Plastic objects that are not containers, such as toys, hangers and toothbrushes, go in the garbage. |
| R18 | garbage | Small loose items like straws, plastic cutlery and loose caps fall through sorting equipment, so they go in the garbage. |
| R20 | recycling | Cartons for milk, juice, broth and plant-based drinks go in the blue recycling cart once they are empty and rinsed. |
| R19 | garbage | When you are unsure about an ordinary, non-hazardous item, put it in the garbage rather than risk contaminating the recycling. |

## Topics

### Collection schedule

- **S01** Your collection day depends on the area you live in; check the collection calendar for your area on the waste service's website or app.
- **S02** The green organics cart is collected every week.
- **S03** The blue recycling cart and the black garbage cart are collected on alternating weeks.
- **S04** Set your carts out the evening before or early on the morning of your collection day.
- **S05** Bring empty carts back from the curb by the end of collection day.
- **S06** In a week with a public holiday, collection may be delayed by one day; the area calendar shows any changes.

### Setting out your carts

- **P01** Place carts at the curb with the wheels facing your home and the lid fully closed.
- **P02** Leave space between carts and keep them clear of parked cars, mailboxes, poles and low branches.
- **P03** Do not overfill carts; anything that keeps the lid from closing may be left behind.
- **P04** In winter, place carts on a cleared surface, not on top of snowbanks.

### Missed collection

- **M01** If your cart was not emptied, first check that it was out on time and that it did not have a warning tag.
- **M02** Report a missed collection to the waste service by the end of the next day through the website, app or customer service line.
- **M03** If a cart is damaged, lost or stolen, request a repair or replacement through the waste service.

### Apartments and condos

- **A01** Apartment and condo residents use the shared bins in their building's waste room or collection area.
- **A02** Sorting rules are the same in apartments as in houses: recycling, organics and garbage are kept separate.
- **A03** Ask your property manager or building superintendent where the shared bins and the bulky item area are.
- **A04** Never leave items in hallways, stairwells or beside a full bin; tell the property manager if bins are full.

### Drop-off depots and events

- **D01** Community drop-off depots accept household hazardous waste, electronics and other items that cannot go in carts.
- **D02** Household hazardous waste collection events are also held at different locations through the year; dates are posted on the waste service's website and app.
- **D03** Drop-off of household hazardous waste and electronics is free for residents in normal household quantities.
- **D04** Transport hazardous products upright, sealed and separate from passengers, ideally in the trunk.
- **D05** Check the waste service's website or app for the drop-off location nearest to you and its opening hours before you go.

### Bulky item pickup

- **B01** Residents of houses book a bulky item pickup in advance through the waste service's website, app or customer service line.
- **B02** Set booked bulky items at the curb the evening before the scheduled pickup day.
- **B03** Apartment and condo residents take bulky items to the building's designated bulky item area; each building or complex has its own spot, so ask the property manager.
- **B04** Leaving bulky items at the curb without a booking is treated as illegal dumping.
- **B05** Furniture and appliances in good condition can be donated to charities and reuse centres instead.

### Rules, warnings and fines

- **F01** Contamination means putting the wrong items in a cart, such as plastic bags in recycling or plastic in organics.
- **F02** A contaminated cart may be left unemptied with a warning tag that explains the problem.
- **F03** If your cart is tagged, remove the wrong items and set it out again on your next collection day.
- **F04** Repeated contamination after warnings can lead to a fine.
- **F05** Illegal dumping, including leaving items on curbs, in parks or beside shared bins without permission, can result in a fine.
- **F06** Putting hazardous waste or batteries in any cart is prohibited because it endangers collection workers.

### Reduce and reuse

- **U01** The best way to reduce waste is to avoid it: buy only what you need and choose reusable bags, bottles and containers.
- **U02** Donate usable clothing, furniture, toys and household goods to charities, thrift stores or neighbours.
- **U03** Repair items when you can instead of replacing them.
- **U04** Plan meals and store food properly to cut down on food waste.

### Home composting

- **H01** Residents with a yard can compost at home in a backyard compost bin.
- **H02** A backyard compost bin can take fruit and vegetable scraps, coffee grounds, eggshells, leaves and grass.
- **H03** Keep meat, fish, bones, dairy and oils out of a backyard compost bin; put them in the green organics cart instead.
- **H04** Mix wet green material with dry brown material like leaves, and turn the pile now and then.

### Keeping organics clean

- **O01** To reduce odours, wrap food scraps in newspaper or keep them in the freezer until collection day.
- **O02** Line your kitchen pail or green cart with newspaper or a paper bag; plastic bags of any kind are not allowed.
- **O03** Keep the green cart lid closed and rinse the cart now and then to keep pests away.

### Moving in or out

- **V01** When moving, start sorting early and donate what you no longer need.
- **V02** Book a bulky item pickup well ahead of your move date, or ask your property manager about the building's bulky item area.
- **V03** Flatten moving boxes for recycling, or pass them on to someone else who is moving.
- **V04** Do not leave items behind at the curb or in the building when you move out.

### Seasonal and holiday waste

- **N01** After the holidays, plain wrapping paper and cardboard boxes go in recycling, while ribbons, bows and foil wrap go in the garbage.
- **N02** Real holiday trees go in organics once lights, decorations and the stand are removed.
- **N03** Leaves and garden waste can be set out in paper yard waste bags beside the green cart in spring and fall.

### Why sorting matters

- **W01** Correct sorting keeps recyclables clean so they can be made into new products.
- **W02** Organics are turned into compost instead of going to landfill.
- **W03** Wrong items, especially batteries and plastic bags, can damage equipment, start fires and injure workers.
- **W04** When in doubt about an ordinary item, put it in the garbage; wishful recycling contaminates whole loads.
