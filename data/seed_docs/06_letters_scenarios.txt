Letter to the Editor: Thank You, Collection Crews

To the editor,

I want to thank the collection crews who empty our carts every week in every kind of weather. Last winter I watched a driver carefully work around a snowbank someone had buried their cart in.

I'd like to remind my neighbours to make their jobs easier. Put your carts on a cleared spot, with the wheels facing your home and the lid closed. Keep them away from parked cars and mailboxes. And please, never put batteries or propane tanks in any cart. They can start fires and explode in the trucks.

A grateful resident

Letter from a Grandparent

Dear grandchildren,

When I was young, everything went in one garbage can. Now our town has three carts and I'm learning along with you.

Here is what I have learned so far. The blue cart is for clean things like cans, bottles, jars, paper and boxes. The green cart is for food and leaves, even the bones from Sunday dinner. The black cart is for the rest, like the chip bags you two leave on the counter.

And batteries go to a special drop-off, because they can start fires. You taught me that one.

With love

type: scenario
Scenario: A Bathroom Renovation

A resident is renovating a bathroom. The old vanity, mirror and towel bar come out, along with leftover paint and a box of old cleaning products.

Construction waste from renovations is not covered by the residential guide. However, the household items can be sorted.

The large mirror does not fit in a cart, so it is a bulky item; a small mirror would be wrapped and put in the garbage. Leftover paint and old cleaning products are household hazardous waste and go to a drop-off in their original containers. Old towels can be donated or taken to a textile drop-off.
=====

Scenario: After a Barbecue

The barbecue is over. Here is how to sort what is left.

Leftover food, bread and meat scraps: green cart.
Empty aluminum cans and glass bottles: blue cart, after a rinse.
Foil trays: rinse them and put them in the blue cart. Foil covered in food goes in the garbage.
Charcoal and barbecue ashes: let them cool completely for several days in a metal container, then bag them and put them in the garbage.
Plastic cutlery and straws: garbage.
An empty propane tank: household hazardous waste drop-off.

Scenario: Helping a Neighbour Move Out

A neighbour is moving out of a house and asks for help with the leftovers.

The sofa and dining table are still in good shape, so they are donated to a reuse centre. A broken bed frame is a bulky item, so a bulky item pickup is booked well before moving day.

Clothes that no longer fit are donated. Moving boxes are flattened for the blue cart or passed on to another family.

A box of old phones and a broken laptop goes to an electronics drop-off after the data is wiped. Leftover paint goes to a household hazardous waste drop-off.

Nothing is left at the curb without a booking.

A new tenant moves into an apartment building and has many questions.

Where are the bins? The property manager shows the tenant the shared waste room.
Are the rules different? No. Recycling, organics and garbage are separated just like in a house.
Where do the moving boxes go? Flattened, in the shared recycling bins.
What about the old mattress the tenant replaced? It goes to the building's designated bulky item area.
What if the bins are full? The tenant should tell the property manager rather than leaving bags beside the bins.
