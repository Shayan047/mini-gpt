How to Set Out Your Carts

Setting out your carts correctly helps the crews empty them quickly and safely.

Step one: check your collection day. Each area has its own day, shown on the collection calendar in the app or on the website. The green cart goes out every week, while the blue cart and black cart take turns on alternating weeks.

Step two: set your carts at the curb the evening before or early on the morning of collection day.

Step three: place each cart with the wheels facing your home and the lid fully closed. Leave space between carts and keep them away from parked cars, mailboxes, poles and low branches.

Step four: do not overfill. Anything that keeps the lid from closing may be left behind.

Step five: bring your empty carts back from the curb by the end of collection day. In winter, place carts on a cleared surface, never on top of a snowbank.

Soft Plastic Versus Rigid Plastic

Plastic is the most confusing material in the waste guide, but one question solves most cases: does it hold its shape?

Rigid plastic containers, such as bottles, jugs, tubs, clamshells and takeout containers, hold their shape. Once they are empty and rinsed, they go in the blue recycling cart. Put caps and lids back on.

Soft plastic, such as bags, wrap, chip bags, candy wrappers, frozen food bags and squeeze pouches, is flexible and can be scrunched into a ball. Soft plastic goes in the garbage, never in recycling, because it tangles the sorting machines.

There is one more group: plastic objects that are not containers at all. Toys, hangers, toothbrushes, pens, garden hoses and CDs are plastic, but the blue cart only takes plastic containers. These go in the garbage. Small items like straws and plastic cutlery also go in the garbage, because they fall through the sorting screens.

Paper: When It Is Recyclable and When It Is Not

Clean, dry paper is one of the easiest things to recycle. Newspapers, magazines, office paper, junk mail, envelopes, paper bags, paper egg cartons, cardboard tubes and cardboard boxes all go in the blue cart. Envelopes with plastic windows are fine, and staples can stay on.

Paper stops being recyclable when it gets dirty. Paper soiled with food or grease, like used napkins, paper towels and greasy pizza boxes, goes in the green organics cart, where it can be composted.

A few paper items are exceptions. Shredded paper goes in the green cart, because loose shreds jam the sorting equipment. Receipts go in the garbage, because most are printed on coated paper. Paper coffee cups go in the garbage because of their plastic lining. Greeting cards with glitter, foil, batteries or sound chips go in the garbage too, and so does foil or glitter wrapping paper.

How to Prepare Hazardous Waste for Drop-Off

Household hazardous waste includes products that are flammable, corrosive, toxic or explosive, plus batteries and anything that contains mercury. Here is how to bring it in safely.

Keep each product in its original, labelled container with the lid sealed. Never mix products together, and never combine leftover chemicals into one container.

Tape the terminals of batteries with clear tape so they cannot spark.

Pack everything upright and transport it sealed and separate from passengers, ideally in the trunk.

Before you go, check the waste service's website or app for the nearest community drop-off depot and its opening hours, or for upcoming household hazardous waste collection events. Drop-off is free for residents in normal household quantities.

Common items include leftover paint, paint thinner, pesticides, motor oil, antifreeze, propane tanks, pool chemicals, household cleaners like bleach and drain cleaner, gasoline, fire extinguishers, fluorescent tubes and mercury thermometers.

Why Pet Waste Goes in the Garbage

It seems natural to put pet waste in the green cart along with other organic material, but in Tidewell, pet waste goes in the garbage. Bag it, tie the bag, and put it in the black cart.

The same goes for cat litter, which should be bagged and placed in the garbage. Diapers also go in the garbage, even if they are labelled compostable.

What about pet fur and hair? Those can go in the green organics cart. And old dry pet food can go in the green cart as well, once you take it out of its packaging. Empty pet food cans are recyclable in the blue cart after a quick rinse.

Getting Rid of a Refrigerator

Refrigerators and freezers are bulky items. If you live in a house, book a bulky item pickup in advance through the waste service's website, app or customer service line. When you book, mention that the appliance contains refrigerant so it can be handled properly. Air conditioners and dehumidifiers also contain refrigerant, so mention it for those too.

For child safety, remove the doors or tape them shut before you set the fridge out. Put it at the curb the evening before the scheduled pickup day.

If you live in an apartment or condo, ask your property manager about the building's designated bulky item area.

If the fridge still works, consider donating it to a charity or reuse centre instead.

The Material Rules, Explained

The A to Z guide lists many items, but no list can include everything. That is why the waste guide teaches material rules.

Clean paper and cardboard go in the blue cart. Paper soiled with food or grease goes in the green cart.
Rigid plastic containers go in the blue cart. Soft plastic goes in the garbage.
Metal cans, tins and clean foil go in the blue cart.
Glass bottles and jars go in the blue cart. Other glass, like drinking glasses and window glass, goes in the garbage, wrapped.
Cartons go in the blue cart.
All food scraps and yard waste go in the green cart.
Small items with a plug, cord or battery go to an electronics drop-off.
Batteries and products labelled flammable, corrosive, toxic or poison go to a household hazardous waste drop-off.
Items too large for a cart are bulky items.
Clothing and fabric go to donation or a textile drop-off.
Medications and needles go to pharmacy take-back.
Foam packaging goes in the garbage.

And when you are unsure about an ordinary, non-hazardous item, choose the garbage over the recycling.

Recycling Cans and Foil the Right Way

Aluminum cans and food cans are among the most valuable things in your blue cart. Empty them and give them a quick rinse. For food cans, put the lid inside the can and pinch the top closed so the sharp edge is covered.

Aluminum foil and foil trays are recyclable too, as long as they are clean. Scrunch clean foil into a ball at least the size of a fist so it does not get lost in the sorting equipment. Foil that is covered in food goes in the garbage.

Aerosol cans are a special case. Only completely empty aerosol cans go in recycling. If there is still product inside, the can is household hazardous waste and must go to a drop-off.

Holiday Tree Collection

After the holidays, real holiday trees can go in the green organics cart. Remove all lights, decorations, tinsel and the stand first. You can cut the tree to fit in your green cart with the lid closed, or set it out whole on the holiday tree collection days announced each winter on the website and app.

Artificial trees are not organics. They go in the garbage, or through bulky item pickup if they are too large for the cart.

Your old string lights are electronics. Please take them to an electronics drop-off rather than putting them in any cart.

type: explainer
Contamination: What It Is and Why It Matters

Contamination means putting the wrong items in a cart, such as plastic bags in recycling, food in the blue cart, or plastic in the green cart.

A small amount of contamination can spoil a whole load. Greasy food soaks into clean paper. Plastic bags and cords wrap around machines and stop the sorting line. Batteries can spark fires in trucks and at the sorting facility.

When crews see contamination, they may leave the cart unemptied with a warning tag that explains the problem. If your cart is tagged, remove the wrong items and set it out again on your next collection day. Repeated contamination after warnings can lead to a fine.

The easiest way to avoid contamination is to remember that recyclables must be empty, clean, dry and loose.

How to Donate Instead of Dispose

Before anything goes in a cart or out to the curb, ask whether someone else could use it.

Clothing, shoes, linens, handbags, belts and stuffed animals in good condition can be donated. Make sure they are clean and dry and bring them in a tied bag. Tie pairs of shoes together so they stay matched.

Furniture, bicycles, exercise equipment and working small appliances can go to charities, thrift stores and reuse centres.

Books in good condition, plastic toys, dishes and drinking glasses can all be donated too.

Repairing items when you can, and buying only what you need, are the other two parts of reducing waste.

type: explainer
Electronics Versus Bulky Items

Both electronics and bulky items need special handling, but they go to different places.

Small items with a plug, cord or battery, like phones, laptops, printers, toasters, hair dryers, power tools and headphones, are electronic waste. They go to an electronics drop-off at a community depot, or to a retailer take-back program.

Items too big to fit in a cart with the lid closed are bulky items, even if they plug in. Large appliances like washing machines, dryers, stoves, dishwashers, refrigerators and water heaters are bulky items, and so are treadmills. They need a bulky item pickup that you book in advance, or your building's bulky item area if you live in an apartment.

Preparing Recyclables in Under a Minute

Empty it. Pour or scrape out anything left inside. Food scraps go in the green cart.
Rinse it. A quick rinse is enough; containers do not need to be spotless.
Cap it. Put caps and lids back on bottles, jugs and jars.
Flatten it. Break down cardboard boxes so they fit with the lid closed.
Keep it loose. Never bag your recyclables.
Keep it dry. Wet paper and cardboard are hard to recycle.

That's it. Your blue cart is ready.

Cartons Belong in the Blue Cart

Milk cartons, juice cartons, broth and soup cartons, and juice boxes are all recyclable in the blue cart. Cartons are made of layers, but the recycling system is set up to handle them.

Empty each carton and rinse it, then put the cap back on. For juice boxes, push the straw inside the box. Do not flatten cartons with liquid still in them; empty them first.

type: explainer
Foam Is Garbage

Foam packaging, sometimes called styrofoam, is not recyclable in Tidewell. Foam takeout containers, foam cups, foam egg cartons, foam blocks from electronics boxes, and packing peanuts all go in the garbage.

Scrape food out of foam containers first. Break large foam blocks so they fit in the cart with the lid closed. Bag packing peanuts so they do not blow away. Many shipping stores will take clean packing peanuts for reuse.

Note the difference between foam egg cartons and paper egg cartons: paper egg cartons are recyclable in the blue cart.

type: announcement
Notice: Lithium-Ion Batteries and Truck Fires

Collection crews have reported several fires in trucks caused by lithium-ion batteries hidden in carts. Lithium-ion batteries are found in phones, laptops, power banks, cordless tools, vapes and many other devices.

Never put any battery, or any device with a battery, in a cart. Remove batteries from devices when you can and tape their terminals with clear tape. Take batteries to a household hazardous waste drop-off and devices to an electronics drop-off.

Putting hazardous waste or batteries in any cart is prohibited because it endangers collection workers.

Clearing Out After a Party

The party is over and the kitchen is a mess. Here is how to sort it.

Leftover food, used napkins and greasy pizza boxes go in the green organics cart. Empty cans and glass bottles go in the blue cart after a quick rinse. Plastic cups and plastic takeout containers that hold their shape are recyclable once they are empty.

Plastic cutlery and straws go in the garbage. So do chip bags, candy wrappers and foam cups. Paper coffee cups go in the garbage, but their plastic lids are recyclable.

type: explainer
What Is Household Hazardous Waste?

Check the label. If a product is labelled flammable, corrosive, toxic or poison, it is household hazardous waste. These products can harm workers and the environment, and they can cause fires or leaks in a collection truck.

Household hazardous waste never goes in any curbside cart, and it should never be poured down a drain. Bring it to a community drop-off depot or a household hazardous waste collection event.

Batteries of every kind are also household hazardous waste, as are items that contain mercury, like fluorescent tubes, CFL bulbs and old mercury thermometers.

A product that is completely used up may be fine elsewhere. For example, a completely empty, rinsed plastic cleaner bottle can go in recycling, and a completely empty, dried-out paint can can go in the garbage.

Keeping Your Green Cart Fresh

Odours and pests are the main complaints about green carts, and both are easy to manage.

Wrap food scraps, especially meat and fish, in newspaper. Or keep scraps in the freezer until collection day.
Line your kitchen pail or green cart with newspaper or a paper bag. Never use plastic bags, not even compostable ones.
Keep the green cart lid closed at all times.
Rinse the cart now and then.
Set the green cart out every week, since it is collected weekly, even if it is not full.

Reporting a Missed Collection

If your cart was not emptied on collection day, first check two things. Was the cart out on time? Did it have a warning tag? Tagged carts are left because they contained items that do not belong.

If the cart was out on time and was not tagged, report the missed collection to the waste service by the end of the next day, through the website, the app or the customer service line.

If your cart is damaged, lost or stolen, you can request a repair or replacement the same way.

Textiles Do Not Go in the Blue Cart

Clothing, shoes, bed sheets, towels, curtains and other fabric items never go in the recycling cart. Fabric tangles the sorting machinery, just like plastic bags and hoses.

Wearable and usable items should be donated. Worn-out, torn or stained textiles can still go to a textile drop-off bin, where they can be turned into new fibres. Make sure everything is clean and dry and in a tied bag.

type: howto
Disposing of Ashes and Cigarette Butts Safely

Hot ashes cause cart fires every year. Fireplace ashes, barbecue ashes and charcoal must cool completely for several days in a metal container. Once they are cold, bag them and put them in the black garbage cart.

Cigarette butts must be fully extinguished before disposal. Soak them in water to be sure they are out, then bag them and put them in the garbage.

type: explainer
Fats, Oils and Grease

Never pour cooking grease or oil down the drain. It hardens in pipes and causes blockages.

For small amounts, let the grease cool and harden, or soak it up with paper towel, and put it in the green organics cart. Bacon fat and frying oil can be handled the same way.

Large amounts of used cooking oil should go to a drop-off depot.

Motor oil is different. It is household hazardous waste and must be taken to a household hazardous waste drop-off in its original container.
