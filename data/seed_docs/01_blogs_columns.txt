My First Month Sorting Waste in Tidewell

When I moved to Tidewell, I thought recycling was simple: if it looked like plastic, it went in the recycling. I was wrong about half the time. The first lesson came in my second week, when my blue cart was left behind with a warning tag. The tag explained that plastic bags are not allowed in recycling. They tangle the sorting machines, so they go in the garbage instead. I had been bagging all my recyclables like a tidy person. It turns out recyclables have to go in loose.

The second lesson was about my kitchen. I had no idea that every kind of food scrap goes in the green organics cart, including meat, bones and dairy. Now I keep a small pail by the sink lined with newspaper, and I empty it into the green cart every few days. Greasy pizza boxes and used paper towels go in there too.

My advice to other newcomers: look at what the item is made of. Clean paper and cardboard go in the blue cart. Food and dirty paper go in the green cart. Soft plastic and foam go in the black cart. And anything with a plug or a battery goes to an electronics drop-off.

Ask the Sorter

Dear Sorter, my partner insists that a coffee cup is paper and belongs in the recycling. I say it goes in the garbage. Who wins? - Cup Confused

Dear Cup Confused, you win this round. Paper coffee cups have a thin plastic lining that keeps the coffee from soaking through, and that lining means they cannot be recycled. Put the cup in the black garbage cart. Your partner gets partial credit, though: the rigid plastic lid can go in the blue recycling cart. Better yet, bring a reusable mug next time and skip the question entirely.

Dear Sorter, what do I do with old paint from my basement? - Brushed Off

Dear Brushed Off, leftover paint is household hazardous waste. Keep it in its original, labelled can with the lid sealed and take it to a household hazardous waste drop-off or a collection event. Never pour it down the drain or put it in a cart. If a can is completely empty and dried out, the can itself can go in the garbage.

Five Things I Wish I Had Known About the Green Cart

1. Meat and bones are welcome. I used to throw chicken bones out with the garbage because I thought they were too messy for compost, but that was a mistake. The green organics cart takes all food scraps, including meat, fish, bones and dairy.

2. No plastic bags, not even compostable ones. This surprised me. The green cart does not accept plastic of any kind, including bags labelled compostable. Line your pail with newspaper or a paper bag.

3. Dirty paper counts. Used napkins, paper towels and greasy pizza boxes all go in the green cart, because paper with food or grease on it cannot be recycled.

4. The freezer is your friend. To keep odours down, I keep meat scraps in the freezer until collection day.

5. It gets collected every week. Unlike recycling and garbage, which alternate weeks, the green cart is picked up weekly.

Bonus tip: pet waste does not go in the green cart. Bag it and put it in the garbage.

Why I Stopped Wishful Recycling

I used to be a wishful recycler. If something seemed like it should be recyclable, in it went: plastic hangers, a broken drinking glass, a garden hose, coffee pods. I thought I was helping.

Then I read the waste guide properly. Wishful recycling contaminates whole loads. Plastic objects that are not containers, like hangers and toys, go in the garbage. Q and A with a Collection Crew Member

What is the most dangerous thing people put in carts?
Batteries, without a doubt. Lithium-ion batteries especially. When the truck compacts the load, a battery can be crushed, spark and start a fire. Propane tanks are another one; they can explode. Both belong at a household hazardous waste drop-off.

What makes your job harder?
Overfilled carts. If the lid does not close, things fall out or we may have to leave some behind. Carts placed too close to parked cars or mailboxes are hard for the truck's arm to reach. Please leave space around each cart, with the wheels facing your home.

What do you wish residents knew?
That hot ashes can start a cart fire. Let ashes cool completely for several days in a metal container, then bag them and put them in the garbage. And please, bag your pet waste and put it in the garbage, not the green cart.
=====

Ask the Sorter: Medicine Cabinet Clean-Out

Dear Sorter, I found a pile of expired pills and some old needles from when my dad had diabetes. Can I flush the pills and throw the needles in the garbage? - Worried Daughter

Dear Worried, please don't do either. Unused or expired medications should be returned to a pharmacy through a take-back program. Never flush them or put them in any cart, because they harm people and water. Needles and other sharps must be placed in a sealed, puncture-resistant container, such as a sharps container or a sturdy plastic bottle with a screw lid. Return the sealed container to a pharmacy, or take it to a household hazardous waste drop-off. Never put loose needles in any cart, because they can injure collection workers.

While you are in there: old mercury thermometers are household hazardous waste, and digital thermometers are electronics.

Talking Trash with the Outreach Team

Our outreach coordinator answers the most common questions the team hears at community events.

What is the number one mistake you see?
People keep putting plastic bags in the blue cart, but they do not belong there. Plastic bags, wrap and other soft plastic go in the garbage. If you can scrunch it into a ball, it is soft plastic.

Number two?
Food left in containers. Containers need to be empty and given a quick rinse. They do not need to be spotless, but leftover food contaminates paper and cardboard.

What surprises people the most?
That shredded paper goes in the green cart, not the blue cart. Loose shreds jam the sorting equipment. People are also surprised that receipts go in the garbage, because most are printed on coated paper.

Any final advice?
Learn the material rules. If you know that rigid plastic containers, paper, metal cans, glass bottles and jars, and cartons go in the blue cart, you can sort almost anything.

What Happens After the Truck Leaves

Ever wondered where your carts go? Organics are turned into compost instead of going to landfill. Recyclables are sorted by material so they can be made into new products, which only works if they are clean. Garbage is everything left over.

Sorting mistakes cause real problems. Batteries start fires. Plastic bags, hoses and cords wrap around machines. Food left in containers soaks into paper and ruins it. And hazardous products can injure the workers who handle your carts.

So the next time you rinse a jar or tape the end of a battery, know that it makes a difference. Correct sorting keeps recyclables clean, keeps workers safe, and turns food scraps into compost.

Glass: Not All of It Is Recyclable

This one tripped me up for years. Glass bottles and jars go in the blue recycling cart once they are empty and rinsed, with the lids back on. But other glass does not.

Drinking glasses, window glass, mirrors and glass ovenware like baking dishes are not recyclable. They melt at a different temperature from bottles and jars. They go in the garbage, wrapped in newspaper or put in a box labelled as broken glass so no one gets cut. The same goes for ceramic dishes, mugs and pottery, though if they are still in good condition you can donate them.

Light bulbs are their own puzzle. Regular incandescent bulbs go in the garbage, wrapped. Fluorescent tubes and CFL bulbs contain mercury and are household hazardous waste. LED bulbs are electronics. Who knew?
